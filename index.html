Info: what should students notice?

At each sudden change in the Hamiltonian, the wavefunction ψ(x,τ) does not jump. But the energy eigenstates and eigenvalues change, so the energy probabilities can jump. Between switches, the probability of measuring each energy of the current Hamiltonian is constant, even as |ψ|² evolves.

After removal, the original infinite-well spectrum returns, but the wavefunction is generally a superposition rather than the original φ₂. The final energy probabilities depend on how long the perturbation was on. Reflection symmetry about the midpoint is preserved, so only even-n states of the original well are populated.

Scaled units: a=1, E₁⁽⁰⁾=1, τ=E₁⁽⁰⁾t/ℏ. The perturbation is V′(x)=2V₀|x/a−1/2| with V₀=7E₁⁽⁰⁾. We diagonalize the Hamiltonian numerically in a truncated 24-state infinite-well basis; plotted energies are its numerical eigenvalues. This is an exact unitary evolution within that finite basis, not a first-order perturbation-theory approximation.
